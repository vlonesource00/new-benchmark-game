# VANGUARD racecraft upgrade — 17 October 2026

The deployed configuration now allows close racing and gentle door rubbing. The target is up to **20% accumulated damage over 20 laps**, with a proportionally smaller allowance for shorter races. Repairing the car does not refund damage already spent. This changes the earlier zero-contact objective; clean passing remains reported separately.

## What changed

- **Race-level damage accounting.** A private ledger observes the car's existing damage assignments, including periods controlled by the pit pilot. Its scalar value is sent in VANGUARD's own public state envelope. It survives service handbacks and repairs, and recognises a new race. The accessor preserves the damage value and original physics; no game-engine or shared-controller source was changed.
- **Local spending limit.** Forecast gentle side contacts are priced by incremental normal impact speed. A sustained predicted touch is not charged a fresh full impulse every timestep. Actual contact spending can trigger a two-second recovery period before the driver uses its close-racing allowance again.
- **Door contact and nose braking are separate.** A parallel car already beside us can remain beside us without automatically activating the frontal brake or abandoning the move. Hazards, meaningful crossing angles, hard squeezes and heavy predicted impacts retain their guards. This permits rubbing; it does not command the car to make contact.
- **GT3 passing routes use the available pace.** Fresh dry tyres use the normal pace envelope instead of an additional 4% route-margin reduction. Cold/low-grip, wet and worn conditions retain the extra reserve. GT3 nominal/held separations are 2.20/2.08 m. The prototype keeps its proven 2.65/2.35 m corridors: applying GT tuning indiscriminately broke its existing passing checks.
- **Better public-state forecasts for GT3.** Road progression keeps the observed anchor, lateral drift decays smoothly, valid measured acceleration blends into the route model, and simulated shifts include the torque interruption. In the traced worn-traffic case, 1.5 s road-position MAE fell from 2.49 m to 1.71 m. This is a diagnostic on one closed-loop encounter, not a universal accuracy estimate. The prototype retains the earlier forecast setting because the new one regressed its verified pass.
- **Damage affects the physics model.** VANGUARD now models the game's increased drag and reduced engine torque as damage builds up, then restores those physical parameters after repairs while retaining the race's spending ledger.
- **Pit approaches use the correct braking reserve.** The pit profile now receives the same class/condition-dependent braking exponent as the main route. Previously worn GT tyres could use the optimistic default exponent on the in-lap even though the base racing profile had been corrected.
- **A pit call is not an emergency obstacle.** A normally moving car that calls for the pits remains physical and activates frontal braking when needed, but its call alone no longer triggers a sudden evasive corridor. Stopped, sideways or rejoining cars retain hazard treatment. A trace showed the old classification launching an evasion just before VANGUARD's own pit call, leaving it sliding when its pit path arrived.
- **Hazard forecasts cannot recurse.** A rival can be moving in course coordinates while being a hazard in road coordinates. Its inertial forecast now bypasses the route-model dispatcher instead of calling back into itself. The diagnostic trace exposed this boundary case; a permanent regression covers it.
- **Pass statistics survive pit stops.** Service resets discard transient route/encounter state while preserving the race's accumulated pass book. A new race clears it.

The core design remains Argon's: a physics-rate host controller, asynchronous corridor planning, C2 route contracts, the original game physics and separate strategy. No neural-network training or GPU jobs were used.

## Endurance verification

The live-strategy test uses Harbor Ring, GT3, 20 laps, standing starts, seed 7 and the fixed grid INTERCEPT / RAZOR / APEX / VANGUARD. The independent recorder sums all positive damage increments, including damage subsequently repaired. It also distinguishes pit-related damage, contacts on the nose, rear strikes, side rubbing and severe impacts.

An early run exposed a real integration bug: service resets erased the driver's internal damage budget even though the independent recorder retained 9.62%. The ledger fix was then rerun over all 20 laps and matched the recorder exactly. That run also exposed the worn-tyre in-lap issue addressed by the pit-profile correction.

The first pit-profile correction alone did not eliminate the failure: `upgrade-20lap-final-candidate-2026-10-17.json` completed with 8.42% accumulated damage and one illegal lap. A subsequent trace identified the evasive response to the other car's pit call. After the classification fix, the targeted 325 s rerun reached the earlier failure without damage. This is why the first pit-profile change is not presented as a complete fix on its own.

Final-source endurance reports are `results/upgrade-accepted-20lap-control-2026-10-17.json` and `upgrade-accepted-20lap-candidate-2026-10-17.json`. They supersede earlier endurance runs for acceptance. An unrelated INTERCEPT strategist update landed between the earlier reports and these checks, so the final control and candidate both use the same current opponent source; do not directly attribute cross-revision race-time differences to VANGUARD.

It stayed comfortably under the user's damage allowance. This is one fixed-seed endurance comparison, not an established reliability rate or race win: its 0.34 s time difference is negligible, and the upgraded car still finishes fourth. Its own maneuver book records six attempts and one retained pass against two attempts and none for the control; those driver-book counts are not the independent encounter scoreboard.

The runs took 74.0 s and 72.4 s of wall time sequentially. In the candidate's Node lockstep fixture, the local controller's p95 was 0.050 ms and planner p95 was 1.63 ms. These are CPU fixture timings, not rendered-browser frame latency.

The 20% target is a planning and acceptance budget, not a change to the game's damage rules or a guarantee against other cars striking VANGUARD. Pit laps are reported separately from illegal laps. Overall race time and finishing position are also reported separately: taking fewer stops or changing another driver's race can improve one without improving the other.

## Verification and rejected changes

All 13 regression groups pass on the final runtime source. They cover the damage budget, repair/handback lifecycle, new-race reset, physical damage model, contact classification, stale routes, exact short physics prediction, wet/worn surfaces, learning contamination, maneuver geometry, prototype passes, latency, traffic, pit-approach classification and the recursive hazard boundary. The production game build also passes; its existing large-chunk warning remains.

The mixed-grid check retains the original zero-contact conservative control. Racing mode permits only classified gentle side contact, no nose hits or severe impacts, no illegal track use and at most 3% accumulated damage for its three-lap setup. It does not silently waive the old safety test.

Longer held corner corridors and broader early-commitment rules were tested and removed: they added no useful passing result and could hurt the race start. Applying the new GT corridor/forecast settings to the prototype was also rejected. There is one current racer configuration, with calibrated class settings, rather than multiple deployable experiment arms.

Benchmarks run one below-normal-priority CPU child at a time. Large reports and traces stay local and are ignored by Git. Source/harness hashes, actual starts, options and code revision are saved with the results.
